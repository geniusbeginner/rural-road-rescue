# 1b. The "2023 study"

**Claim as it circulated:** "a 2023 study identifies critical roads from flood-induced failures and loss of hospital access."

**What the sources say**
- **Title:** Critical facility accessibility and road criticality assessment considering flood-induced partial failure
- **Authors:** Utkarsh Gangwal, A. R. Siders, Jennifer Horney, Holly A. Michael, Shangjia Dong
- **Journal:** *Sustainable and Resilient Infrastructure*, vol. 8, issue sup1, pp. 337–355
- **Dates (Crossref):** published online **2022-11-25**; print issue **2023-01-30**
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.1080/23789689.2022.2149184 (OpenAlex marks it open access, "hybrid")
- **Metadata source:** Crossref API, `api.crossref.org/works/10.1080/23789689.2022.2149184`
- **Abstract source:** OpenAlex API, `api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1080/23789689.2022.2149184`. OpenAlex stores abstracts as a word index, which I rebuilt in order, so this is a secondary copy of the publisher's abstract. The publisher page (tandfonline.com) returned HTTP 403 with a bot challenge ("Just a moment... Enable JavaScript and cookies to continue"), so I didn't go further there.
- **Abstract excerpts (OpenAlex):**
  - "This paper examines communities' accessibility to critical facilities such as hospitals, emergency medical services, and emergency shelters when facing flooding."
  - "We use travel speed reduction to account for flood-induced partial road failure."
  - "A modified betweenness centrality metric is also introduced to calculate the criticality of roads for connecting communities to critical facilities."
  - "The proposed model and metric are applied to the Delaware road network under 100-year floods."
  - "We also identified critical roads that are vital for post-flooding access to critical facilities."

**What I conclude (my inference, not the source's)**
- This matches every part of the claim: critical roads, flood-induced failures, and access to hospitals and other critical facilities. The "2023" fits the print issue; the online version appeared in November 2022.
- Several things may differ from how the claim was presented:
  - The study area is **Delaware, USA**, not India.
  - Failure is modelled as **partial** (reduced travel speed), not road removal.
  - Criticality comes from a **modified betweenness centrality**, which is the screening metric our brief says not to present as the result.
- I can't be sure this is the specific study the claim meant; it's the closest title and abstract match I found.
